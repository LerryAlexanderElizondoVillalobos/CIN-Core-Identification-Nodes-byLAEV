# The Individual as a Foundational Unit of Participation

**Project:** CIN — Core Identification Nodes  
**Author:** LAEV — Lerry Alexander Elizondo Villalobos  
**Class:** Foundational concept  
**Status:** Conceptual; technical requirements pending  
**Version:** 1.0

## 1. Purpose

CIN examines how individuals can participate in decentralized infrastructures without making every aspect of participation dependent on a single institution or centralized service. The individual is treated as a foundational subject of participation. This does not mean that a person and a technical node are the same thing.

## 2. Keep person, registration, and node distinct

CIN's Foundational Principles establish three separate concepts:

- **Person:** a human being with a distinct registration and credential lifecycle.
- **Node:** an independently operated technical participant with its own identifiers, configuration, cryptographic material, operational records, and lifecycle.
- **Association:** a defined relationship between a person and a node, requiring explicit creation, verification, update, and termination rules.

Creating or operating a node does not, by itself, register a person. A person's registration must not depend permanently on one device or one node. A person may interact with more than one node or service over time, subject to applicable protocols and authorization rules.

## 3. Individual participation

An individual-oriented architecture should investigate capabilities such as access to and administration of permitted personal records; presentation of selected evidence or credentials; authorization of defined operations; participation in multiple communities and institutions; continuity when devices, service providers, or institutional relationships change; and correction, review, and dispute procedures.

The technical design must define which records are authoritative, who may issue or verify claims, and how a person can recover access without treating a single device as the person.

## 4. Participation across social structures

Individuals participate in overlapping structures: families, local communities, organizations, territorial administrations, and national or international institutions.

CIN can investigate interoperability between these structures through independently defined nodes and protocols. The model should not assume that every relationship forms a strict hierarchy or that a higher-level node automatically controls an individual participant.

A family or community may provide support and context. Institutions may issue or verify records within their competence. The individual remains a distinct participant, subject to applicable law and clearly defined responsibilities.

## 5. Initial registration and developing autonomy

A person's registration may begin at birth through legally recognized procedures and authorized representatives. Any technical representation must preserve the distinction between the person, the registration record, the representative, and the node or service used to manage records.

As the individual develops capacity, the system should support an appropriate and legally compliant transition in representation and control. Exact age thresholds, authority rules, consent models, and safeguards are jurisdiction-dependent and remain to be specified.

## 6. Design principle

**Begin the participation architecture with the individual as its foundational subject, while keeping the human person, the registration record, and the technical node explicitly distinct.**

This principle guides research. It does not imply that every individual must operate a physical server, hold a particular device, or disclose all personal information.

## 7. Relationship to existing CIN principles

This document supplements, and does not replace, CIN_FOUNDATIONAL_PRINCIPLES.md. Where terminology or requirements differ, the existing distinction between person, node, and association remains controlling until a reviewed architectural decision changes it.
