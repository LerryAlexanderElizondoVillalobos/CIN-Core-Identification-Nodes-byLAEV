# Individual Lifecycle and Initial Registration

**Project:** CIN — Core Identification Nodes  
**Author:** LAEV — Lerry Alexander Elizondo Villalobos  
**Class:** Lifecycle concept and requirements input  
**Status:** Proposed; legal and technical rules pending  
**Version:** 1.0

## 1. Purpose

CIN investigates how a person's registration and participation can remain continuous through different stages of life. This document focuses on initial registration, including registration at birth, and the progressive transition from authorized representation to individual administration.

## 2. A lifecycle perspective

A registration infrastructure should not assume that the primary user is always an adult with personal devices, technical knowledge, or direct access to institutional services.

A possible lifecycle model is:

1. **Birth and initial registration:** a birth event is recorded through legally recognized procedures and authorized actors.
2. **Childhood:** records may be administered by legally authorized representatives, with access and disclosure limited by purpose and law.
3. **Developing capacity:** the system supports age-appropriate participation and transition of authority as required by applicable law.
4. **Adult or legally independent participation:** the individual can administer permitted records, credentials, and authorizations directly.
5. **Continuity over time:** records remain referable and correctable as devices, institutions, jurisdictions, and circumstances change.

These stages are conceptual. Legal capacity and representation differ across jurisdictions and must not be assumed to be identical everywhere.

## 3. Separate the relevant records and actors

An implementation must distinguish the person to whom a record relates; the birth or other source event; the registration record and its issuer; the legal representative, if any; the technical node or service used to manage or reference records; the association between the person and a node; and credentials and permissions issued for specific purposes.

A node's creation is not a person's registration. A technical identifier is not proof of a person's civil status or attributes.

## 4. Requirements to investigate

Future specifications should address accepted sources and provenance for birth-event claims; authorized registrars and representatives; consent, notice, and age-appropriate participation; correction, dispute, appeal, and duplicate resolution; privacy, retention, and selective disclosure; accessibility and assisted procedures; continuity and recovery when a representative, device, or provider changes; transition of control when legal capacity changes; and compatibility with competent civil-registration authorities.

## 5. Inclusion and safeguards

Lack of a device, connectivity, technical literacy, or existing document must not be treated as proof that a person does not exist. A technical network must not become the sole gateway to fundamental rights or essential services.

The system should support assisted and offline procedures where feasible, while clearly recording provenance and verification status.

## 6. Status and non-goals

This document does not establish a universal legal obligation to register through CIN, define a global age of autonomy, or replace civil-registration law. It records requirements for future research and specification.
